MyFolio vs Wix: which is better for a developer portfolio?

Wix is the biggest general website builder, and for a business site its drag-and-drop editor is hard to beat. But a developer portfolio is not a business site — it should show your code. Here's how the two compare on the things that matter for a portfolio: code ownership, GitHub, looks, and price.

What Wix does well

Wix is the biggest general website builder, and that size buys three real strengths. First, the drag-and-drop editor: for a business site, a store, or a restaurant page, it is the fastest way to a finished-looking site without touching code. Second, the free plan costs $0 — you can build and publish on a Wix subdomain with Wix ads and pay nothing while you decide. Third, Wix has AI site-creation tools that draft a first version of a site for you. None of that makes it a developer portfolio tool, but for a general website it is genuinely good at what it does.

Where MyFolio differs

The difference is what the site is built from and who owns it. Wix builds a site you design by hand inside Wix, and the site stays inside Wix — its own help center says it can't be exported or hosted elsewhere, so leaving means rebuilding from scratch. MyFolio builds your portfolio from your GitHub repos automatically, renders it as an interactive 3D site instead of a static template, and on paid plans lets you download the full source code as a ZIP for ₹149 once — and host it anywhere, including free on GitHub Pages. You pay once for the code; you don't rent the site for $17 a month.

The verdict

Wix wins if you want a general website — a business, a store, a restaurant — with a drag-and-drop editor and a free plan to start. MyFolio wins for a developer portfolio: it is generated from your GitHub, it looks like a 3D experience instead of a template, and you can download the full source as a ZIP for ₹149 once instead of paying $17 a month forever. The deciding question is simple: do you want to own your portfolio, or rent it?
